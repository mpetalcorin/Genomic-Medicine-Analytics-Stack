# DATASHEET.md# Datasheet, Simulated Genomic Medicine Datasets (Notebook-Generated)## MotivationThese datasets are generated to support a reproducible, governance-oriented demonstration of real genomic medicine analytics workflows, without requiring access to human subjects genomic data.## CompositionThe notebook generates multiple simulated datasets:### 1. Genotype cohort (population genomics)- **Rows:** individuals (n ≈ 1,800)- **Columns:** SNPs (m ≈ 12,000)- **Encoding:** genotype dosage in {0, 1, 2}, with missingness injected then imputed by SNP means for modelling- **Structure:** LD blocks (correlated SNP clusters) + ancestry-specific allele-frequency shifts- **Metadata:** ancestry group labels (A0, A1, A2), QC metrics (call rate per SNP and per individual)### 2. Trait dataset (statistical genetics)- **Outcome:** binary trait with:  - polygenic component (hundreds of small-effect SNPs)  - ancestry-correlated confounding term (to stress-test correction)  - noise term- **Covariates:** genetic PCs (top components), PRS (derived from training GWAS)### 3. GWAS summary table- SNP identifier, z-score, chi-square, p-value, and genomic position (simulated coordinates)- Outputs used for Manhattan plot, QQ plot, and top-hit reporting### 4. Fine-mapping proxy dataset (credible set)- Approximate Bayes factors and posterior probabilities for SNPs in the lead locus window- Credible set membership for a target posterior mass (e.g., 95%)### 5. Tumour mutation catalogue (cancer genomics)- **Tumours:** n ≈ 260- **Channels:** 96 (trinucleotide-context style channelization)- **Generative process:** mixtures of k underlying signatures with tumour-specific exposures, plus overdispersed total mutation burden (TMB)- Output includes recovered signatures and exposure matrix from NMF### 6. VAF tables for clonality proxy- For each tumour, a list of somatic VAF values- Generated as mixtures of 1–3 components with distinct mean peaks, then analysed with Gaussian mixture models### 7. Variant “annotation-like” feature table (AI-assisted prioritisation)Features mimic signals used in practical triage:- CADD-like deleteriousness proxy- Conservation proxy- Gene constraint proxy (LoF intolerance-like)- Splicing disruption proxy- Missense severity proxy- Regulatory impact proxy- Population MAF proxy- Tumour-context VAF proxy- Ancestry one-hot features (for auditing)Labels are generated from a latent risk function, with an injected mild ancestry-correlated artefact to force auditing behaviour.## Collection processNo human samples are collected. All data are generated in-code.## Preprocessing / cleaning / labeling- Missing genotypes are injected intentionally and then imputed by SNP-wise mean (for demonstration simplicity).- GWAS uses PC adjustment (top PCs).- PRS is computed from training GWAS effects (simplified derivation).- NMF signatures use nonnegativity constraints and RMSE-vs-k diagnostics.- Variant labels are synthetic and should be treated strictly as demonstration targets.## Uses### Suitable uses- Teaching genomics QC, PCA/t-SNE structure, GWAS diagnostics, and PRS evaluation- Teaching cancer genomics concepts (mutational processes, TMB heterogeneity, clonality intuition)- Teaching evaluation of AI systems beyond AUC (calibration, interpretability, group-wise auditing)- Dry-run of reporting outputs and governance checklists### Unsuitable uses- Any clinical or patient-facing inference- Any claim of clinical validity or real disease association- Benchmarking methods as if they were tested on real cohorts## DistributionThe notebook regenerates datasets at runtime. - cohort summary tables (CSV)- top GWAS hits tables- credible set tables- tumour exposures matrix- variant feature table## Privacy and ethics- No personal data- No identifiability risk- No ethics approval required for simulated data## Known limitations- Simulation cannot replicate all complexities of real sequencing pipelines (batch effects, platform artefacts, contamination, relatedness).- Simplified modelling choices are intentionally educational and governance-oriented rather than “best possible” for production.## References (PubMed-indexed, foundational methods and governance)Price, A.L., Patterson, N.J., Plenge, R.M., et al.
